Mini demo · Manipulator kinematics & control

Droid Arm Reach

Click (or click & drag) anywhere and C-3PO’s arm will follow a smooth, straight trajectory to reach it. Hover to see a preview of the pose. Don’t touch anything and C-3PO will plan and follow a few poses by himself.

What you’re looking at

A robotic arm usually cares about where it needs to go in Cartesian space, otherwise known as “up–down, left–right, forward–back” space. But robotic joints are commanded to specific motor angles. The mapping between those two domains is half the battle in manipulator dynamics and control.

In this case, C-3PO’s arm has three joints (shoulder, elbow and wrist), but its hand only cares about two things: left–right and up–down. Every click runs three small problems back to back:

  • Plan. Draw a straight line to the goal and decide how fast to move along it. The hand speeds up and slows down smoothly, to lower the time and effort to get there, and to avoid crashing into the object it’s grabbing.
  • Pick the joints. At each instant, work out what the joints must do to keep the hand on that line. The spare joint goes toward keeping the arm in a relaxed pose.
  • Push. Work out how hard each motor has to push, accounting for gravity and for the way swinging one link yanks on the others.

The hard part is the edge of its reach limit. With the arm nearly straight, no joint can move the hand any farther out, and the naive math asks the motors for infinite speed. The arm has to see that coming and give up a little accuracy instead of flailing.

Things to try

  • Click near the edge of the reach and watch λ on the readout climb as the arm straightens. That’s the arm trading accuracy for sane motor speeds.
  • Drag the goal around mid-reach. The path bends smoothly instead of jerking onto a new line.
  • Watch the error charts during a long reach. The joints lag their reference and the hand drifts several millimetres off the line, then settles, but not quite to zero: the real arm is heavier than the controller thinks, so it sags a little at rest.
